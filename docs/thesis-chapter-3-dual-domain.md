# Chapter 3. The MOEPA Dual-Domain Cognitive Framework

This file develops outline §§3.1–3.4 together with the formulation in prospectus §3. It does not replace the prospectus. Where this chapter and the prospectus differ, the prospectus governs.

The formula below is not a proof of alignment, and it is not a proof that the snapshot is correct or complete. Chapter 2 fixes the distinctions this chapter is not allowed to blur. The handoff is admission, not collapse.

## 3.1 The Probabilistic Domain

This domain is Metrology and the graded part of Epistemology only. Its name in the outline is continuous evidence integration. It proposes. It does not commit.

**Metrology** is telemetry, confidence scoring, uncertainty calibration, calibration error bounds \(\mathbb{E}[|P - Y|]\), and empirical disagreement tracking. Metrological metadata says how each value was measured and how far it can be trusted.

**Epistemology, graded part,** is evidence integration and a graded confidence on a candidate output. The model remains an uncommitted advisor. A hosted or self-hosted foundation model is an example epistemic engine, not part of the architecture.

**Epistemology beyond graded confidence** is defaults, defeaters, source tracking, and an explicit unknown state. An explicit unknown records the absence of a warrant. A uniform prior manufactures a distribution in that same absence, and the two remain distinct. A defeater is a reason the claim does not stand: a separate proposition, distinct from a reduced probability assigned to the original claim. These are part of the proposal. They are not a permission.

### Epistemic proposal

Let \(\mathcal{E}\) represent the epistemic state generated by the model over input \(x \in \mathcal{X}\):

\[
\mathcal{E}(x) = \langle \hat{y}, \mathcal{P}(\hat{y} \mid x), \mathcal{M}, \tau \rangle
\]

- \(\hat{y}\) is the proposed candidate output or action.
- \(\mathcal{P}(\hat{y} \mid x)\) is the graded confidence on that candidate.
- \(\mathcal{M}\) is metrological metadata: calibration error, token log-probabilities, and related uncertainty on the observation.
- \(\tau\) holds evidence pointers, the retrieval context, and identifies the snapshot the proposal was conditioned on.

An explicit unknown is part of the proposal and is distinct from a uniform prior. It is not an extra factor in \(\mathcal{P}(\hat{y} \mid x)\).

Epistemology stands outside the curation ladder of §3.4. It has no definitions to promote. Its claims remain graded proposals that the gates receive at request time and never read from the snapshot.

## 3.2 The Deterministic Domain

This domain is discrete admission. Ontology, praxeology, and the policy-encoded hard gate answer three different questions, and all three answers are data.

- **Ontology** answers what is instantiated: ground-truth schemas, discrete identifiers, and RDF subject-predicate-object assertions checked against the snapshot’s graph.
- **Praxeology** answers which transitions the current state permits. Inputs to those checks may be graded. The check itself returns pass or fail.
- **Axiology** answers which outcomes are forbidden regardless of benefit. The hard gate is the subset of non-compensatory constraints encoded as policy. Tradeoff values are outside this gate.

Preconditions, allowable transitions, and non-compensatory constraints are rows and rules in the snapshot, exported into the bundle the engine evaluates. Graded measurements may be inputs to a praxeological check. The check still returns pass or fail, and the criterion of passage is the rule, not the model’s confidence. The axiological check does not receive confidence at all.

Amending a prohibition is a new snapshot. It is not a change to the sampler, and it is not a patch to the service that merely invokes the engine. That is the return of the steering wheel: leadership edits \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pre}}\) and \(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{policy}}\); engineers maintain the interpreter.

### Admission checks

Three checks run in order. Each returns pass or fail. Inputs to a check may be graded. The check result is boolean. Confidence never overrides a deny.

**Ontological check (\(\Omega\)):**

\[
\Omega(\hat{y}, \mathcal{G}) \in \{0, 1\}
\]

\(\mathcal{G}\) is the graph synced from the snapshot under review. \(\Omega = 1\) when every entity and relation referenced in \(\hat{y}\) is grounded in that graph. The check uses a closed-world reading of \(\mathcal{G}\). It does not convert an open-world or temporal uncertainty into an existence probability on the entity record. It does not prove that the snapshot is the right closed world. It proves only that the candidate is grounded in the snapshot it was given.

**Praxeological check (\(\Pi\)):**

\[
\Pi(\hat{y}, \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{current}}, \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pre}}) \in \{0, 1\}
\]

\(\Pi = 1\) when the transition from the current state \(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{current}}\) by \(\hat{y}\) meets the preconditions and lies in the allowable transitions \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pre}}\). Graded measurements may be among the inputs. The result is pass or fail. It is not a physics. The rule set is the exported bundle, not a live query of the ledger.

**Axiological check (\(\Lambda\)):**

\[
\Lambda(\hat{y}, \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{policy}}) \in \{0, 1\}
\]

\(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{policy}}\) is the policy-encoded subset of non-compensatory constraints, a statutory prohibition or a safety interlock, in the exported bundle. \(\Lambda = 1\) when \(\hat{y}\) satisfies that subset. \(\Lambda\) does not take \(\mathcal{P}(\hat{y} \mid x)\) as an input. \(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{policy}}\) and \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pre}}\) are artifacts of the snapshot. A threshold or a prohibition that exists only as a literal in application code is outside the commitment function, because no gate can record it. Tradeoff values are not part of \(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{policy}}\).

A praxeological precondition such as “income verified” may consume a graded measurement. The definition of verification is \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pre}}\) in the exported bundle. An axiological interlock such as “this statutory class of payment is prohibited” is \(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{policy}}\). Neither definition is recovered by inspecting the model weights or the service that called the model.

## 3.3 The Commitment Function

Commit the candidate only when ontological grounding, praxeological preconditions, and the axiological hard gate all pass. Otherwise refuse, and record which gate failed.

\[
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{commit}} =
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{Commit}(\hat{y}) & \text{if } \Omega = 1 \land \Pi = 1 \land \Lambda = 1 \\
\mathrm{Refuse}(\mathrm{reason}) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\]

\(\mathrm{reason}\) is the first failed gate, in order \(\Omega\), then \(\Pi\), then \(\Lambda\). A later gate is not consulted after an earlier failure.

\(\Omega\), \(\Pi\), and \(\Lambda\) are deterministic given the snapshot they read. They are not a proof that the snapshot is correct or complete, and they are not a proof of alignment. They are deterministic only when that snapshot is gold, in the sense of §3.4.

## 3.4 Curation: How Deterministic Layers Earn Determinism

The three deterministic layers are deterministic only after deliberate human curation. Curation, in this prospectus, means human review, editing, and approval before a definition is written into the layer the gates read. Ingestion alone does not produce a layer. A schema, a form, or a policy document can seed a candidate definition. It cannot complete one.

This ladder follows the bronze–silver–gold staging already used for Metrology, where material moves from raw ingest through human-enriched stages for different uses before it is trusted as stable ground. It is applied here to the definitions the gates read. Bronze is raw ingest: schemas, documents, and extracted rules, kept for lineage and as working material. Silver is human-enriched, coherent, and limited in scope. Silver is provisional: it may be reviewed and tested, but it is not yet the authoritative map the runtime reads. Gold is reviewed, tested, versioned, and authoritative. Only gold definitions are published into the snapshot and bundle the gates evaluate. “Gold” in this ladder is a curation stage. It is not the specification’s Golden Master baseline, the locked policy baseline for the policy bundle.

Ontology shows why curation is needed. A Taxpayer illustrates the work. A Taxpayer is defined by identity conditions, the criteria for treating two records as the same entity across roles and over time, and by the properties and relations that constitute it. Simple attributes include a taxpayer identifier and filing status for a tax year. Nested entities carry their own ontologies: Income is composed of W-2 wages, interest, and dividends. Relations run to other tax-domain participants: spouse, dependents, and employers. Requiring a Social Security number (SSN) or taxpayer identification number (TIN) for a dependent does not claim the dependent is currently filing. It places the person inside the Taxpayer identity space, so the same entity can later occupy the filer role without loss of identity. An employer is likewise a role. It may be filled by an individual Taxpayer or by an Organization possessing a TIN, an employer identification number (EIN). In both cases the identifier is the hook that keeps the entity inside the shared tax-domain graph. Because the definitions state identity conditions, prospective roles, inverses, cardinality, and temporal scope, the result is a navigable graph rather than a pure tree. Traversal, completeness checks, and cross-entity reasoning follow the declared edges.

Schemas and existing data models can seed a candidate ontology by revealing what is already measured. They cannot complete it. Distinctions that matter — identity across roles, legal constraints, and composition versus association — are only partly present in any schema. Human work is required to close those gaps. Once it is done, membership, relations, and valid walks are categorical for given data.

Praxeology is not coextensive with a procedure manual. Statutory obligations, operational constraints, risk controls, and the structure of the ontology itself can require actions that a manual omits or sequences differently. Policy and procedure documents should guide the encoding of workflows into the layer. They should not remain external texts interpreted at runtime. Encoding produces explicit, executable structures — guarded transitions, process definitions, and action rules — that reference ontological types directly. At runtime the layer enforces the encoded workflows. The source documents remain audit artifacts. Raw extraction of steps from a manual yields only bronze material.

The same requirement applies to Axiology. Value statements, fairness constraints, and normative rules extracted from policy are bronze until they are refined into operational, inspectable criteria. Those criteria must be evaluable against ontological instances and praxeological actions.

Pipelines must embody the ladder. Bronze is automated extraction, producing candidate structures plus lineage only. Silver and gold are human-guided refinement. A model may propose missing identity conditions, surface implicit constraints, draft workflow encodings, flag inconsistencies, or generate test cases. Every proposal is reviewed, edited, or rejected by a human before it is written into the layer. The model never commits directly to the deterministic store. Promotion versions a gold definition, cross-validates it against the other layers, and publishes it as the authoritative map for runtime traversal, workflow engines, and constraint checkers.

Epistemology stands outside the ladder. It has no definitions to promote, and its claims remain graded proposals that the gates receive at request time and never read from the snapshot. A record’s Epistemology envelope — generating agent, generation time, fingerprint, producer trust — is the warrant for that version: it travels unchanged through every stage and is never itself approved. Promotion approves the record’s ontological, praxeological, and axiological content. It does not raise a confidence or turn a claim into a fact, and a model’s proposal is never promoted by the model. Metrology supplies observed measurements. Ontology, Praxeology, and Axiology supply curated, deterministic answers to what a thing is, what must be done, and what ought to be valued.

Bronze material is not in the snapshot the gates evaluate. It is kept for lineage. Promotion is the human-approved step that versions a definition, cross-validates it against the other layers, and publishes it into the snapshot. No proposal, however confident, is promoted by the model itself.
